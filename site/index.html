How to read the rankings

Only regular plans are shown. Funds are compared only with others in the same SEBI category. The top 10 changes with the measure you pick above; the number beside each fund is its place in the whole category, so it stays the same when you filter by fund house.

  • Rolling consistency leans on how often the fund beat its category's typical return, plus its typical rolling return and how rarely it lost money.
  • Smallest falls leans on the worst fall in the last 5 years, how long it took to recover, and how often a holding ended in a loss.
  • Long-term return uses the typical 5-year rolling return, then the main rolling period.
  • Risk-adjusted return leans on the 3-year Sortino and Sharpe ratios and how much of the index fund's falls the fund took.
  • Build your own lets you weight return, consistency and falls yourself.

Funds without enough history for the category's main holding period (1 year for debt and arbitrage funds, 3 years for the rest) are left out of the top 10 unless you tick “Include funds too new to judge”.

How each measure is worked out
  • Rolling return: the yearly return over every possible 1, 3, 5, 7 or 10-year period, one per trading day, from AMFI's daily NAVs. “Typical” is the middle value; worst and best are the extremes.
  • Beat category: the share of month-ends when the fund's rolling return was higher than the middle fund in its category on that date.
  • Chance of loss: the share of all holding periods of that length that ended below the starting value.
  • Worst fall: the biggest drop from a previous high in NAV. “Recovery” is the time from the bottom back to that high.
  • Crash replay: the fund's biggest fall inside each market episode, next to the typical fall in its category.
  • SIP range: a monthly SIP started on the first NAV of every possible month, valued on the first NAV after 3, 5 or 10 years. The return shown is the XIRR.
  • Money in hand: an estimate after capital gains tax for FY 2026-27 and inflation. Equity-oriented funds: 12.5% on long-term gains above ₹1.25 lakh a year; debt-oriented funds: your slab rate; others: 12.5% after 24 months. Surcharge and cess are left out.
  • Volatility: how much the NAV moves up and down in a year, from daily changes over 3 years.
  • Key ratios: from month-end NAVs over the last 3 or 5 complete years. Standard deviation is the spread of monthly returns times √12. Sharpe is the yearly return above the risk-free rate divided by standard deviation; Sortino divides by the spread of the falls only. The risk-free rate is the typical overnight fund's monthly return (from AMFI NAVs). Beta, alpha, R-squared, capture ratios, tracking error and information ratio compare the fund with the index fund that tracks its category's usual benchmark, named on each fund page.
  • Portfolio: read from each fund house's monthly portfolio file (SEBI format). Company size uses AMFI's list of listed companies by average market cap. “What changed” compares the number of shares or units held with the previous month, so price moves are not counted as buying or selling.
  • AMFI risk check: AMFI's monthly disclosure for mid cap and small cap funds: days needed to sell 25% and 50% of the portfolio, the share held by the top 10 investors, portfolio PE against the benchmark, turnover and AMFI's own standard deviation and beta.
Words used on this site
  • NAV: the price of one unit of the fund, published by AMFI every working day.
  • Regular plan: the plan you buy through a mutual fund distributor. Its expense ratio includes the distributor's commission.
  • Rolling return: the yearly return over every possible period of the same length (for example every 3-year period), not just from one start date. It shows what investors who started on different days actually got.
  • Expense ratio: the share of your money the fund takes each year to run the scheme. It is already taken out of the NAV.
  • Large, mid and small cap: SEBI ranks listed companies by market value. The top 100 are large caps, 101 to 250 are mid caps, and the rest are small caps.
  • Standard deviation (ups and downs): how much the fund's returns swing from month to month. Lower means a steadier ride.
  • Sharpe ratio (return for the risk taken): extra return over a safe overnight fund for each unit of ups and downs. Higher is better.
  • Beta: how much the fund moves when the market (here, an index fund) moves by 1%.
  • Down capture (share of market falls taken): in months when the index fund fell, how much of that fall this fund took. Below 100% means it fell less.
  • Portfolio overlap: how much of two funds' money is in the same companies or bonds.
  • Macaulay duration: the average time, in years, for a bond fund to get its money back. Longer duration means the NAV moves more when interest rates change.
Where the data comes from

NAVs are for growth options of the regular plans of open-ended schemes. Where a scheme has old retail or institutional plans, only the current plan is shown. NAV history runs from April 2006 at the earliest, so “since data start” may be later than a fund's launch.
